# Vani redesign: a quiet place for your voice

Owner request: rebuild Vani's product design using Wispr Flow Dictation and Notetaker as references, while preserving working dictation. Date: 2026-09-12.

## Research and evidence boundaries

Reviewed Wispr's current official product pages, desktop navigation reference, meeting editor reference, and hub reference. Direct browser inspection covered the Dictation and Notetaker landing pages and the public dictation demo. The authenticated desktop app and real meeting recording were not exercised. Some animated website sections rendered blank after navigation in this browser; do not mistake their DOM content for a visually verified product screen. This is product research plus a public-surface design review, not a claim of having tested every Wispr feature or beaten its accuracy.

### Dictation

Wispr pairs an ambient recording control with a management hub. The hub separates history, dictionary, snippets, style and notes. Its desktop navigation reference describes customizable shortcuts, recovery actions and optional persistent bar visibility. That separation is useful: speaking should not require visiting a dashboard. Its broader hub also introduces several destinations and account actions that Vani does not need. [Official navigation reference](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/5096240724-navigating-the-wispr-flow-app-desktop-ios-and-android).

The public demo uses familiar writing tasks and a visible hold-key instruction to explain the product. This is more immediately useful than a feature tour. Vani should put its actual configured shortcut beside the action, explain permission purposes, and reveal recovery actions when a transcript exists. [Public demo](https://wisprflow.ai/demo).

The Dictation landing page uses warm cream, dark text, editorial serif headings and a restrained lavender action. My eye goes first to its headline, then the download action, then the animated voice graphic. The lesson is confident hierarchy and generous space. Vani uses its own forest-green palette, system typography and native controls; copying a marketing page wholesale into a utility would waste space. [Dictation](https://wisprflow.ai/).

### Notetaker

Wispr markets bot-free meeting capture, speaker identification, summaries, catch-up questions and search across meetings. Its product is substantially broader than a scratchpad. These are vendor descriptions, not independently measured claims. [Notetaker](https://wisprflow.ai/notetaker).

Its editor documentation makes a particularly good distinction: personal notes, transcript and generated summary remain independent. User edits take precedence over background generation, an existing summary survives failed regeneration, and live versus completed meetings open different relevant views. This is an architectural lesson as much as a visual one: source material and interpretation should never overwrite each other. [Meeting editor reference](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/9406970664-meeting-notes-and-the-editor-in-notetaker-beta).

Its hub organizes meetings for retrieval and continuation. Vani adopts a searchable meeting library, with authored notes, transcript and derived summary kept separate. [Hub reference](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/9247277218-the-notetaker-hub-your-meetings-in-one-place-beta).

## Product decision

Three approaches were considered against the owner's existing direction:

1. Reskin existing controls: smallest change and low risk, but keeps the cramped notebook and fragmented visual hierarchy.
2. Rebuild the presentation layer around the existing domain boundaries: selected. Gives every surface a coherent identity, improves writing and navigation, and keeps proven audio and storage behavior.
3. Rewrite the engine and build a cloud meeting suite immediately: broadest feature scope, highest regression and privacy risk, and no evidence that replacing working speech code improves this user's experience.

The user explicitly asked to proceed and emphasized preserving reliability. The redesign therefore uses approach 2 without another approval round. "From scratch" applies to the visual hierarchy and presentation architecture. Existing correctness is retained as an asset.

## Implemented experience

- **Menu:** compact wordmark and readable state; one prominent dictation instruction; the configured key shown as a keycap; transcript actions grouped only when available; Meetings, Notes and Settings remain immediate destinations. An active meeting replaces the dictation instruction with a return-to-meeting action.
- **Setup:** explain why each macOS permission is needed before its Allow action. Local model preparation and recovery remain explicit.
- **Meetings:** explicit start/stop; microphone and Mac audio; live local transcript; a writing canvas beside source and generated views; local summary with decisions, actions and quotes; saved chunk recovery.
- **Notes:** warm sidebar, title and text previews, searchable library, clear Notes/Recently Deleted categories, spacious editor, readable line spacing, quiet toolbar, export and reversible deletion in contextual actions. Hide the sidebar for focused writing. Command-N creates; Command-F finds; Command-S saves. New notes focus the title. Saved, unsaved, opening and failed-storage states are distinguished.
- **Settings:** persistent navigation rail and a clear heading for each destination. All prior dictionary, learning, snippet, history and diagnostics operations remain available. General controls are grouped into Dictation and On this Mac.
- **Teach:** focused correction canvas with the same typography and palette, while retaining its native focus activation and failed-save behavior.
- **Recording:** compact pill with distinct recording, processing, insertion and recovery messages. It remains non-activating and mouse-transparent.

## Presentation architecture

```text
AppCoordinator ── session / permissions / settings / existing actions
      │
      ├── MenuContentView ── quick dictation and recovery
      ├── SettingsView ── preferences and local vocabulary
      ├── OverlayController ── non-activating status only
      ├── TeachWindowController ── explicit correction lifecycle
      └── NotesWindowController ── native lifecycle and close guard
                  └── NotesView ── search / navigation / editing / export
                           └── NotesModel ── draft and persistence coordination
                                      └── NoteStore actor ── atomic local files
```

`VaniTheme` contains only presentation tokens and two small shared views. NotesModel, NotesView and NotesWindowController are separate files with concrete responsibilities; no generic router, event bus, service registry or additional package is introduced. AppCoordinator can be constructed without auto-start for native UI fixtures; production startup defaults remain identical.

The existing dictation speech engine, focus targeting, clipboard ownership, insertion verification and notebook storage schema are retained. Meeting capture adds a separate store and lifecycle, with exclusive access to the existing speech recognizer and a local Ollama summary client. Category switching uses the existing save barrier, so a failed write cannot silently dismiss a draft. No data migration is needed.

## Design review passes

1. **Information architecture:** shortcut first in the menu; note title/body first in the notebook; stable destination labels in settings. No metrics dashboard or promotional cards.
2. **State coverage:** setup, preparing, listening, processing, insertion and recovery use existing session states. Notes distinguishes loading, empty library, search empty, saved, dirty, deleted and storage failure. Failed drafts remain editable/exportable.
3. **Journey:** launch to a clear hold-key instruction; speak in the current app; see truthful delivery feedback; optionally preserve the thought as a note. Note creation needs one action and immediately provides a cursor.
4. **Visual restraint:** two warm neutral surfaces, one accent, system sans for controls and body, system serif for editorial headings. No gradients, ornamental dashboards, stock illustrations or invented statistics.
5. **Consistency:** same palette, spacing rhythm, wordmark and native control behavior across surfaces. Error meanings retain text and symbols as well as color.
6. **Accessibility and sizing:** system text colors; labeled icon actions; native keyboard-capable windows; Reduce Motion remains supported by the recording animation; opaque backgrounds avoid dependence on transparency. Notebook is checked at 980×680 and 720×480, light and dark. VoiceOver and real hardware dictation remain manual acceptance checks.
7. **Meeting scope:** the user clarified that note taking means a live meeting assistant and chose all-local processing. The implementation now includes capture, transcription and summaries. It does not claim per-person speaker identification, pause/resume, calendar integration or cross-meeting chat.

## Approved meeting architecture

See [MEETINGS_DESIGN.md](MEETINGS_DESIGN.md) for the capture lifecycle, persistence contract,
local model, error paths and practical limits. ScreenCaptureKit supplies microphone and Mac
audio. Completed chunks are persisted before local transcription; notes remain independently
editable. Summaries run through local Ollama with a fixed loopback endpoint and no remote fallback.

## Acceptance and comparison

A defensible "better" claim needs matched tasks, not design adjectives. Compare time to first successful dictation, insertion failure/recovery rate, time to create/find/recover a note, keyboard completion, transcription error rate and cold/warm latency on the same audio and hardware. This release improves observable UI and notebook behavior; it does not establish superiority across Wispr's platforms, languages or meeting intelligence.

Validation receipts and current screenshots live in the release verification directory. The source suite covers existing dictation behavior plus the new notebook category and keyboard flows. Native screenshots supplement functional tests; neither alone establishes zero regressions in every target app.
